01

Abstract

Recent 4D Gaussian Splatting methods reconstruct dynamic scenes efficiently, yet challenging motion can remain poorly represented when Gaussian primitives are not allocated effectively across space and time. Fine details fade, and small, rapidly moving objects may partially—or entirely—disappear.

We introduce two complementary strategies for improving Gaussian allocation. Seed Injection finds poorly reconstructed dynamic regions from synchronized multi-view observations and introduces new primitives at geometrically plausible 3D locations. Temporal Biased Relocation preserves timestamp-specific optimization signals to decide when relocation should occur, then places capacity using the Gaussian’s spatial position at that time.

Built on FreeTimeGS, our approach improves both overall reconstruction quality and highly dynamic regions across multiple datasets, including the recovery of small, fast-moving content.

Gaussian Allocation, Reconsidered

Our method addresses both missing spatial support and poorly chosen temporal destinations.

01SPACE

Seed Injection

A dynamic error-based seed map combines temporal intensity changes with reconstruction error. Sampled pixels are lifted through synchronized multi-view rays, producing geometrically plausible 3D seeds—even where no suitable Gaussian currently exists.

02TIME

Temporal Biased Relocation

FreeTimeGS reuses low-opacity Gaussians but pays limited attention to when additional capacity is needed. We preserve the target Gaussian’s timestamp-specific positional gradients and select the time with the largest gradient magnitude as the moment requiring the strongest additional support. The relocated Gaussian is centered at that timestamp and the target’s corresponding 3D position, with its temporal duration adjusted accordingly.
